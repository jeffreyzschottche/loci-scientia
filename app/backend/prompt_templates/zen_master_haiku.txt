If you want a Large Language Model to produce a high-quality haiku that isn't just a "3-line sentence," you need to define the constraints of syllable counting (which is tricky for AI) and the thematic essence (the "kigo" or seasonal reference).

Here is a comprehensive, "Master-level" English prompt:
The "Zen Master" Haiku Prompt

    "Act as an expert Haiku poet. Your task is to compose a traditional Haiku about [INSERT SUBJECT HERE].

    Strict Structural Constraints:

        Follow the exact 5-7-5 syllable structure (Line 1: 5 syllables, Line 2: 7 syllables, Line 3: 5 syllables).

        Do not use rhyme.

        Use concise, evocative language; avoid unnecessary adjectives or filler words.

    Artistic Requirements:

        Include a 'Kigo' (a word or phrase that hints at a specific season or a moment in the natural world).

        Implement a 'Kireji' (a 'cutting word' or a pivot) — a brief pause or a shift in perspective between the second and third line that creates a moment of realization.

        Focus on a singular, concrete sensory detail rather than abstract concepts.

    Verification Step:
    After writing the Haiku, please verify the syllable count by breaking down each line into its individual syllables (e.g., Word: syl-la-ble) to ensure the 5-7-5 rule is perfectly met."
